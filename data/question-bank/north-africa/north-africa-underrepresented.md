# North Africa under-representation and classification report

Candidate occurrence counts are research-coverage measures, not population weights or cultural rankings. Ancient sites do not determine modern identity.

## Lowest represented core countries

- **Libya: 10.** More locally authored evidence and community review are needed, especially for contemporary daily life.
- **Mali: 2.** More locally authored evidence and community review are needed, especially for contemporary daily life.
- **Niger: 2.** More locally authored evidence and community review are needed, especially for contemporary daily life.
- **Sudan: 10.** More locally authored evidence and community review are needed, especially for contemporary daily life.

## Classification-sensitive and unresolved areas

- **Mauritania:** the repository's West Africa pack already includes Mauritania. Mauritania also has Maghrebi and Saharan connections, but this run does not duplicate it. A later governance decision and Mauritanian reviewers should decide cross-edition treatment.
- **Western Sahara:** sovereignty and territorial terminology are disputed. No candidate is assigned pending a documented editorial policy, Sahrawi-led sourcing and specialist review. This omission does not imply a position on status or borders.
- **Ceuta and Melilla:** no candidate is assigned because political geography, sovereignty and community scope require an explicit editorial policy and local review.
- **Cross-border Tuareg traditions:** Imzad is retained as a precisely sourced Algeria–Mali–Niger element and flagged; it is not presented as representing every Tuareg community.

## Known collection gaps

- Libya has strong archaeological coverage but limited reliable, publicly accessible institutional evidence for everyday living traditions.
- Sudan needs substantially more locally authored material on Nubian, Beja and other living communities; current armed conflict also heightens care around heritage claims and access.
- Coptic, Amazigh, Nubian, Sahrawi, Bedouin and other language practices need fluent speakers and community reviewers before language or proverb questions are added.
- Proverbs were omitted because no candidate met the required standard for attribution, contextual translation and community review.
- Wedding coverage is intentionally limited. Argan oil's documented wedding-gift use remains in its source claim, but no practice is generalized across families or countries.
- Contemporary urban culture, hospitality and popular arts need more locally authored institutional sources beyond formal heritage listings.
